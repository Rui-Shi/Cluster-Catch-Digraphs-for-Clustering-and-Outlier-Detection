# WP2a -- does the NND spatial-randomness test saturate in high dimension?

Generated by `revision_experiments/48_wp2a_nn_saturation.R --summary` on 2026-08-09 23:32:46.

## Verdict

**The saturation claim is refuted.** Over a square alpha x dimension grid with
all four alpha levels genuinely generated at every dimension, the alpha effect
does not decay toward zero as d grows. It *grows*. No dimension in the tested
grid (d = 2, 3, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100) meets the pre-declared indistinguishability
criterion, and in all four (setting, method) combinations the effect at
d = 50 and d = 100 is larger than at d = 2.

## Why the previous evidence was void

21 pairs of files in `R/NN-test_quantile/` are byte-identical: d = 6,7,8,9 for
the 95/99 pair and d = 11-19, 21-28 for the 99/999 pair. One Monte-Carlo run
was saved under two names. waveform (d=21) and vowels (d=12) -- the two data
sets the claim rested on -- both sit inside that duplicated range, so their
"identical output at 99% and 99.9%" was a file compared with itself.

## Design

* New generator `01i_nn_multiquant_table.R`: one Monte-Carlo run emits every
  alpha, and the raw draw matrices are saved so future alphas are free -- the
  contract the RK generator already had via `$Kest.m`, which NN lacked.
* New tables in `R/NN-test_quantile_n200/`: d in {2,3,5,10,20,50,100},
  alpha in {0.10, 0.05, 0.01, 0.001}, n = 200 per iteration, niter = 10000.
  No existing table was modified.
* Data: uniform-cluster and Gaussian-cluster settings, n = 200, 2 clusters,
  5% contamination, generator bodies from `09_wp3_synthetic.R` with only n
  and d parameterized. 40 replicates, seed = 123 + rep, recorded per row.
* Detectors: UN-MCCD and SUN-MCCD (the NND arm), S_min = 0.0625, threshold
  0.5, always scored through `evaluate(Y, score, threshold)`.

## Saturation criterion (declared before the numbers were looked at)

A (setting, d, method) cell is alpha-indistinguishable when
`mean_BA_range < 0.01` AND `mean_min_jaccard > 0.95`.
With n = 199 and n0 = 10, flipping one true outlier moves BA by 0.050 and
flipping one regular point moves BA by 0.00265, so a mean BA range below 0.01
rules out every outlier-label change and admits at most ~3 regular-point
changes. The Jaccard clause blocks the case where the flagged set churns
while BA happens to stay put.

## Zero quantiles: NN does NOT degenerate the way RK does

Source: `wp2a_nn_zero_quantiles.csv`, column `frac_zero_average_excl_pos1`.
Every generated table has exactly one zero, at position 1, which is the
hand-added structural placeholder in the generator ("the NN distance of a
single point is 0", `NN_Dist_Est.R:34`). Excluding it, the zero fraction is
0.000 at every d up to 100 and at every alpha. The RK envelope's known
collapse (91% zeros at d = 100) has no NN counterpart, so zero-degeneracy
cannot be the mechanism behind any high-dimensional behaviour here.

What *does* shrink with d is the envelope's RELATIVE spread across alpha:

| d | mean envelope at alpha=0.10 | at alpha=0.001 | relative spread | zero frac (excl. pos 1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0.10574 | 0.08367 | 0.2392 | 0.000 |
| 3 | 0.21756 | 0.18621 | 0.1577 | 0.000 |
| 5 | 0.40915 | 0.37004 | 0.1013 | 0.000 |
| 10 | 0.68614 | 0.64800 | 0.0574 | 0.000 |
| 20 | 0.91298 | 0.88265 | 0.0339 | 0.000 |
| 50 | 1.11338 | 1.09387 | 0.0177 | 0.000 |
| 100 | 1.20805 | 1.19466 | 0.0112 | 0.000 |

The envelope does compress: the four alpha levels sit 23.9% apart at d = 2 and 1.1% apart at d = 100.
That is a real high-dimensional effect on the *envelope*. It does not
translate into saturation of the *detector*, because the observed NN
statistic concentrates at least as fast, so the test decision stays close to
the envelope and a small shift in the envelope still moves it.

## Cross-check against the on-disk production tables

Part A (`wp2a_nn_crosscheck_tables.csv`): an NN table's entry x is the
envelope for a sample of size x, drawn fresh, so it does not depend on the
table's own n. The fresh n = 200 tables reproduce the production n = 5000
tables entrywise over x = 5..200 to a median relative difference of
0.0007-0.0030 with correlation 0.984-0.999 at d = 10 and d = 20 -- the only
two dimensions where genuinely distinct 99/999 production files exist.

## What the result does and does not say

It says: alpha still binds at every tested dimension. That is the part the
saturation claim denied, and it is settled by the set-identity columns, not
by BA. `frac_reps_identical` is 0.47-0.85 for the uniform setting at d <= 10
and 0.00-0.32 at d >= 20; `mean_min_jaccard` falls from 0.87-0.94 to
0.25-0.60 over the same range. The flagged set moves MORE with alpha in high
dimension, not less.

It does not say that alpha is a well-behaved knob at high d. Mean BA itself
falls from ~0.99 at d = 2 to 0.63-0.68 at d = 100, so part of the growing BA
range is a detector that has become unstable rather than a test that has
become more discriminating. The honest reading is that the high-dimensional
problem is the opposite of the one claimed: alpha matters more where the
detector is weakest, which makes the manuscript's unexplained per-d alpha
schedule harder to defend, not easier.

## Cost

Source: `wp2a_nn_cost.csv`. NN generation at n = 200, engine `orig_list`,
8 contended cores: 0.0115 s/iteration at d = 2, 0.0129 at d = 5, 0.0148 at
d = 10, 0.0209 at d = 20, 0.0377 at d = 50, 0.0736 at d = 100. The measured
anchor for the production tables was 2.355 s/iteration at n = 1000, d = 5 on
4 cores, so dropping to n = 200 buys roughly two orders of magnitude. The
whole seven-dimension, four-alpha table set cost about 31 minutes of
wall-clock; d = 100 was run as two independent 5000-iteration parts to fit a
600 s per-invocation budget.
